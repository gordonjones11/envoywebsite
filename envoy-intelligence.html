Tier 3 Transformation:   Envoy Process Intelligence

One intelligence layer. Two ways your mill uses it.

Envoy Process Intelligence serves two audiences on one shared context layer: operators, continuously, in the rhythm of the control room; and engineers, on demand, through runbooks that turn a week-long investigation into a repeatable workflow. It's running in a mill today.

Request a Demo The Intelligent Factory

The Problem

Every investigation starts from scratch.

A recurring problem shows up: moisture variation, a run of breaks, a felt that isn't lasting. An engineer pulls historian trends, compares grades and speeds, reviews scanner and lab results, checks maintenance history, and asks operators what changed. Two weeks later there's an answer. Six months later the same problem returns, and most of that work is rebuilt from the beginning.

The same analysis, rebuilt

The method lives in one engineer's head and the code lives in one engineer's folder. The next investigation reconstructs both, and rarely reaches the same depth.

Answers arrive after the decision

By the time a study is finished, the grade has run, the roll has been changed, or the outage has been scheduled. Late analysis is expensive analysis.

Alarms without context

Twenty alarms fire from one upset. The control room sees twenty problems, not one event, and the alarms that matter get lost in the ones that don't.

Knowledge that walks out

What to check first, which combination of symptoms points where, which fix worked last time. Most of it is tacit, and none of it is written down anywhere the next shift can find it.

The Platform

One context layer. Two workflow surfaces.

Generic AI gives expert answers about a machine you don't have. Envoy is built the other way around: context first, AI second. The same configured understanding of your mill drives both an always-on operator surface and an on-demand engineering surface, and everything either one learns goes back into the layer underneath.

Operational Intelligence

Twenty alarms are not twenty problems.

Operational Intelligence runs continuously: a scan of every tag every fifteen minutes, measured against the centerlines that define what good looks like right now. What the control room sees is not a wall of alarms but a short, ranked list of what is actually off.

Deviations, grouped into events

One upset can fire twenty alarms across four systems. Related deviations are grouped into a single operational event, so the crew is looking at one problem with a cause, not twenty symptoms competing for attention.

Causes, ranked and cited

Each event carries the likely causes in order, and the next-best actions to take, drawn from your mill's own SOPs and troubleshooting procedures with a citation back to the source. Cross-shift answers stop depending on who is on shift.

Tier-2 alarm management

Your DCS keeps the Tier-1 react-now alarms where they belong. Tier-2 alerts route to Envoy Process Intelligence instead, where grouping and context cut the noise that trains crews to ignore alarms in the first place.

Advisor-first, by design

The Envoy Advisor explains what a piece of equipment does, how it interacts with the rest of the line, and what has worked before, supporting operator judgment rather than replacing it. There is no closed-loop control, and there won't be.
